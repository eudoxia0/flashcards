T: ligand
D: A substance that binds to a biomolecule to produce some biological change

---

T: enteral administration
D: Administration of a drug through the intestines, either orally or rectally

---

C: Oral administration is a subtype of [enteral] administration.

---

C: Rectal administration is a subtype of [enteral] administration.

---

T: parenteral administration
D: Administration of a drug through any route other than the intestines

---

T: intraperitoneal administration
D: Administration of a drug into the abdominal cavity

---

T: bioavailability
D: The fraction of a drug administered that reaches circulation

---

T: prodrug
D: A drug that is converted into an active metabolite in the body.

---

T: dissociation constant
D: the drug concentration at which half of the available binding sites are occupied.

---

Q: What's the notation for the dissociation constant?
A: $K_d$

---

Q: What does $K_d$ stand for?
A: The dissociation constant.

---

C: A [high] value of $K_d$ means the drug has a [low] affinity for the receptors.

---

C: A [low] value of $K_d$ means the drug has a [high] affinity for the receptors.

---

Q: High $K_d$ means low binding affinity. Why?
A: Because you need a higher concentration of the drug to reach the same amount of binding.

---

Q: Low $K_d$ means high binding affinity. Why?
A: Because you need a lower concentration of the drug to reach the same amount of binding.

---

C: $K_d$ is [inversely] proportional to binding affinity.
