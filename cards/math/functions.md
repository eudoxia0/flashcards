T: bijective function
D: a function where every element in the codomain is the image of exactly one element in the domain.

---

C: In a [bijective] function, every element in the codomain is the image of [exactly one] element in the domain.

---

C: A [bijective] function is also called [one-to-one].

---

C: A [one-to-one] function is also called [bijective].

---

T: injective function
D: a function that maps distinct elements in its domain to distinct elements in its codomain.

---

C: In a [injective] function, every element in the codomain is the image of [at most one] element in the domain.

---

C: A function satisfying:

$$
x \neq y \implies f(x) \neq f(y)
$$

is called [injective].

---

C: A function satisfying:

$$
f(x) = f(y) \implies x = y
$$

is called [injective].

---

T: surjective function
D: a function where every element in the codomain is the image of at least one element in the domain.

---

C: In a [surjective] function, every element in the codomain is the image of [at least one] element in the domain.

---

C: A [surjective] function is also called [onto].

---

C: A [onto] function is also called [surjective].

---

T: associativity
D: a property of a binary operation whereby $(ab)c = a(bc)$.

---

Q: Let $\times$ be a binary operation where:

$$
(x \times y) \times z = x \times (y \times z)
$$

What is the name of this property?

A: Associativity.

---

T: commutativity
D: a property of a binary operation whereby $ab = ba$.

---

Q: Let $\times$ be a binary operation where:

$$
x \times y = y \times x
$$

What is the name of this property?

A: Commutativity.
