Definition: [The drug concentration at which half of the available binding sites are occupied.]

---

Q: What's the notation for the dissociation constant?
A: $$K_d$$

---

Q: What does $K_d$ stand for?
A: The dissociation constant.

---

C: A [high] value of $K_d$ means the drug has a [low] affinity for the receptors.

---

C: A [low] value of $K_d$ means the drug has a [high] affinity for the receptors.

---

Q: High $K_d$ means low binding affinity. Why?
A: Because you need a higher concentration of the drug to reach the same amount of binding.

---

Q: Low $K_d$ means high binding affinity. Why?
A: Because you need a lower concentration of the drug to reach the same amount of binding.

---

C: $K_d$ is [inversely] proportional to binding affinity.

C: Term: [Neuron depolarization]

Definition: [An event where a neuron's membrane potential becomes less negative.]

---

Q: What happens when a neuron undergoes a significant depolarization?
A: It triggers an action potential.

Q: Cell membranes are naturally impermeable to ions. Why is this?
A: Ions prefer interacting with polar water molecules than the hydrophobic lipid groups in the cell membrane.

Q: Cell membranes are impermeable to ions. Why do ions get thorough?
A: Because of ion channel proteins.

Q: Evolutionarily: why do neurons maintain an ionic gradient?
A: To maintain an extracellular environment similar to seawater.

C:
Term: [Ionic Gradient]
Definition: [The difference in the concentration of a particular ion between the interior and exterior of a cell.]

Q: What are the mechanisms by which neurons maintain the membrane potential?
A: Ion channels and ion pumps.

Q: When a neuron is at its resting membrane potential, what happens to the net flow of ions across the cell membrane?
A: The cell is at equilibrium and the net flow is zero.

Q: What would happen to the membrane potential if the cell membrane was infinitely permeable?
A: The potential would reach equilibrium at zero volts.

Q: Why is the equilibrium potential of a neuron non-zero?
A: Because the cell is selectively permeable.
