Q: What is the equation for Coulomb's Law?
A: Given two charges $q_1$ and $q_2$, separated by $d$, the magnitude of the electric force between them is:

$$
F = \frac{k_e q_1 q_2}{d^2}
$$

where $k_e$ is Coulomb's constant.

Q: What is electric potential?
A: Electric potential at a point $P$ is the amount of work done bringing a $1C$ charge from infinity (where potential is zero) to $P$ in an electric field.

Q: What is electric potential measured in?
A: Volts.

Q: What is voltage in terms of potential?
A: The difference in electric potential between two points.

Q: What is the term for the difference in electric potential between two points?
A: Voltage.

Q: What is the unit of voltage?
A: The volt.

C: A voltage exists whenever [positive and negative charges are separated].

Q: What is resistance?
A: A measure of how much an object impedes the flow of electric current.

Q: What is the symbol of resistance?
A: $$\Omega$$

Q: What is the unit of resistance?
A: The ohm.

Q: What is resistance a function of?
A: The material, shape, and size of an object.

Q: What is resistivity?
A: An intrinsic property of a material that determines how much it resists electric current.

Q: What is the symbol for resistivity?
A: $$\rho$$

Q: What is the unit of resistivity?
A: $$\Omega \times m$$

Q: What is the unit of conductance?
A: The siemens (S).

Q: What is conductivity?
A: The reciprocal of resistivity.

Q: What is the equation form of Ohm's Law?
A:
$$
I = \frac{V}{R}
$$

Current is voltage divided by resistance.

Q: What is the Coulomb constant analogous to?
A: The gravitational constant.

Q: What is voltage in terms of work?
A: The amount of work done when a $1C$ particle moves from $A$ to $B$.

Q: What is the term for the amount of work done when a $1C$ particle moves from $A$ to $B$?
A: Voltage.
